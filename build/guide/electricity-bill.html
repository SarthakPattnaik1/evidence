Electricity problem — high or wrong bill, disconnection, meter, or new connection delay

Electricity disputes go to the distribution company, then its Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum (CGRF), then the state Electricity Ombudsman.

Answer a few questions and get your list →

What the law says

Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, 2020 (amended 2024) set service standards. Disconnection for non-payment needs at least 15 clear days' written notice (Section 56, Electricity Act, 2003), and old arrears generally cannot be recovered after two years unless shown continuously as due. "Theft" assessments under Section 135 are serious — see an advocate.

Papers that usually help

  • Last 12 months of bills
    A sudden jump is the main proof of a wrong bill.
  • Consumer / connection number and the owner's ID
    Every complaint needs it.
  • Payment receipts for recent bills
    Shows you are not in default, or what was paid under protest.
  • Photos of the meter reading with date
    Compares against the billed reading.
  • Request for meter testing and its report
    A faulty meter requires bill revision.
  • Disconnection notice (if any)
    No proper notice makes the disconnection challengeable.
  • New connection application and acknowledgement
    Delay beyond the timeline can earn compensation.
  • Inspection report, provisional assessment and seizure memo
    Needed to object within the time given.
  • Complaint number and reply from the company
    Needed before CGRF.
  • File with the CGRF (forum) of your discom
    Free consumer forum for electricity disputes.
