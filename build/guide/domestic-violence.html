What the law says

The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 lets a Magistrate grant protection, residence, money, custody and compensation orders. Protection Officers and legal aid can help you file.

Law checked 2026-10-30 Draft — not yet reviewed by a named advocate.

Papers that usually help

  • A private written record of incidents — dates, what happened, who saw it
    Courts look at incidents over time. Notes made close to the time help.
  • Medical records, prescriptions and injury photos
    Independent proof of injuries.
  • Threatening messages, voice notes or emails
    Shows threats in their own words.
  • Copies of earlier complaints (police, helpline, Protection Officer, Domestic Incident Report)
    Shows the abuse was reported.
  • Proof you lived in the shared home (ID with address, letters, photos, ration card)
    Supports a residence order.
  • Marriage certificate or other proof of marriage/relationship
    The Act applies to relationships in a shared household.
  • List and photos of your jewellery, gifts and belongings held by them
    Court can order their return.
  • Your income and expenses, and their income if you know it
    Court can order maintenance and medical/ loss expenses.
  • Children's birth certificates, school records
    Needed for a custody order.
  • Your ID, bank card, phone and important documents — kept with you or a trusted person
    Makes it possible to leave safely if needed.

Questions Pehlo will ask you

  • First: are you safe right now?
  • Who is causing the harm?
  • What has happened?
  • Where are you living now?
  • Are children affected?
  • Have you seen a doctor about any injuries?
  • Have you told anyone official?
  • Do you have your own money and documents with you?
