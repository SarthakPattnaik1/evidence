A client or buyer hasn't paid my business

You have more routes than a regular suit: the MSME council, a commercial suit, arbitration or insolvency. The records decide which is best.

What the law says

Micro and small suppliers must be paid within the agreed period (never more than 45 days), with compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate on delay. Commercial disputes of ₹3 lakh or more go to commercial courts, usually after mandatory pre-institution mediation. Insolvency against a company needs a default of at least ₹1 crore.

Papers that usually help

  • Contract, purchase/work order and any amendments
    Defines scope, price and payment terms.
  • All invoices, with dates and GST details
    The core of the claim.
  • Proof of delivery/acceptance (delivery challans, sign-offs, completion emails)
    The 45-day clock and your claim run from acceptance.
  • Ledger of the client's account, payments received and balance
    Shows exactly what is due.
  • Emails/messages agreeing terms or promising payment
    Fills gaps where nothing is signed.
  • Udyam registration certificate
    Needed for the MSME council route.
  • File a delayed-payment reference on the MSME ODR portal
    Councils can mediate, arbitrate and award interest at three times the bank rate.
  • Ledger confirmations, part-payments or emails admitting dues
    Strong evidence and may extend the time limit.
  • The client's complaints and your replies
    Decides which remedies are realistic.
  • Legal demand notice and proof of delivery
    Usually the first formal step.
  • Debtor company details: CIN, registered office, directors, charges
    Needed for notices, suits and insolvency.
  • GST returns showing the invoices
    Corroborates the supply; the client's input credit records may also show acceptance.

Questions Saboot will ask you

  • Who owes you?
  • How much is unpaid (approximately)?
  • Is your business registered as a micro or small enterprise (Udyam)?
  • What do you have in writing?
  • Has the client raised any complaint about your work?
  • Does the contract have an arbitration clause?
  • When was payment due?
  • Has the client admitted the dues (ledger confirmation, part-payment, email)?
