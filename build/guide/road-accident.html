What the law says

Compensation claims go to the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT). The 6-month filing limit in Section 166(3) of the Motor Vehicles Act has been stayed by the Supreme Court (November 2025) while its validity is decided — still file as early as possible.

Papers that usually help

  • FIR copy and the police accident report (DAR)
    Primary evidence of negligence and vehicles involved.
  • Get an FIR registered and note the number
    Claims rely heavily on police records.
  • Other vehicle's number, photos, driver details and insurance
    The insurer is made a party.
  • Hospital records: MLC, discharge summaries, bills, prescriptions
    Basis of medical compensation.
  • Disability certificate from a medical board
    Major factor in compensation.
  • Income proof: salary slips, ITR, business records, employer letter
    Basis of loss-of-income/dependency.
  • Age proof of the injured/deceased (school certificate, Aadhaar, PAN)
    Age affects the multiplier used for compensation.
  • Death certificate, post-mortem report, legal heirs' details
    Needed for a death claim.
  • Spot photos/videos, CCTV request, witness names
    Supports the claim on negligence.
  • Vehicle repair estimate, bills, surveyor report
    Basis of damage claim.
  • Your own insurance policies and claim papers
    Coordinate claims.
  • Your driving licence, RC, insurance and PUC (if you were driving)
    Insurers check these to avoid liability.

Questions Pehlo will ask you

  • What happened?
  • When was the accident?
  • Is the other vehicle known?
  • Was a police FIR registered?
  • Was the injured or deceased person earning?
  • Has any permanent disability been assessed?
  • Do you have your own vehicle/health insurance that may also pay?
