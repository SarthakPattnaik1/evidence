What the law says

Under the BNSS, an arrested person must be told the grounds of arrest, can have a relative or friend informed, can meet a lawyer, and must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. For many offences police issue a notice to appear instead of arresting. Seized items, including phones, must be listed in a seizure memo.

Papers that usually help

  • Time, place, police station, and officers' names or badge numbers
    Needed for any complaint or bail.
  • Arrest memo, seizure memo, notice or FIR copy
    Your lawyer needs these first.
  • Make, model, IMEI of the seized phone and the seizure receipt
    Needed to apply for its return.
  • Names and numbers of people who saw it
    Supports your version.
  • Your ID and, if driving, licence, RC and insurance
    Most stops end quickly when papers are in order.
  • ID of the person detained and a family member's contact
    Needed for bail and visits.
  • Contact a lawyer or legal aid (15100) for bail
    Bail and production deadlines are short.

Questions Pehlo will ask you

  • What happened?
  • Is this happening right now?
  • Were you given any paper (memo, notice, FIR copy)?
  • Did anyone ask for money?
