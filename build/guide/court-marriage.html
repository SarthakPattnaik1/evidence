What the law says

Under the Special Marriage Act, 1954 one partner must have lived 30 days in the district before giving notice; the notice is displayed for 30 days for objections, and the marriage needs three witnesses. Hindu marriages can be registered after the ceremony under state rules. The Supreme Court (Shakti Vahini, 2018) directed police to protect couples facing threats and set up safe houses. Some states (e.g. Uttarakhand) require registration of marriages and live-in relationships.

Papers that usually help

  • Age proof for both (birth certificate, Class 10 certificate or passport)
    Required for any marriage registration.
  • Photo ID of both (Aadhaar, voter ID or passport)
    Required for registration and any police request.
  • Existing marriage certificate or registration number
    Needed to get a copy or correction.
  • Contact details of a trusted friend, advocate and the local police station
    Useful in an emergency and for any protection order.
  • Address proof showing 30 days' residence in the district
    Needed for the Special Marriage Act notice.
  • Passport photos of both
    Attached to the application.
  • Proof of single status (affidavit; divorce decree or death certificate if previously married)
    A prior subsisting marriage makes a new one void.
  • Three witnesses with ID and address proof
    Required at registration.
  • Wedding invitation, photos and ceremony proof
    Needed to register a marriage already done.
  • Threat messages, call recordings, names of those threatening
    Supports a protection request to police or High Court.
  • Written request to the Superintendent of Police for protection
    Courts expect you to approach police first.
  • Passport, visa and no-impediment/single-status certificate from the embassy
    Required for a foreign national.
