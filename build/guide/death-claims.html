What the law says

RBI's 2025 directions require banks to settle deceased depositors' claims within 15 calendar days of receiving all documents, with compensation for delay. Since 1 November 2025, accounts can have up to four nominees. A nominee receives the money, but the legal heirs remain entitled to it under succession law. A succession certificate (civil court) is often needed for debts and securities when there is no nominee; a legal heir certificate is issued by the state revenue office.

Papers that usually help

  • Death certificate (several attested copies)
    Every claim needs it.
  • Your ID and KYC, and the deceased's ID
    Required by all institutions.
  • List of accounts, policies, PF/UAN, folio and property details
    Lets you claim everything; nothing is missed.
  • Nomination records
    Simplifies the claim.
  • Legal heir certificate or family tree / heirship affidavit
    Needed where there is no nominee, and for pension and property.
  • No-objection letters from the other heirs
    Lets one person claim without a court.
  • The will (original) and probate if required
    Decides distribution where valid.
  • EPF death claim forms (PF, pension and EDLI insurance)
    EPFO pays PF, family pension and EDLI insurance.
  • Insurance policy document and claim form
    Basis of the insurance claim.
  • Property title documents and tax receipts
    Needed for mutation in heirs' names.
  • Vehicle RC and insurance
    Needed to transfer the vehicle.
  • Written complaint about the delay, then escalation (RBI / insurer / EPFO portal)
    Deadlines are enforceable.
