Builder delayed my flat or isn't delivering what was promised

Homebuyer cases rest on the agreement, the payments and the promised date. Let's gather those first.

What the law says

Homebuyers can approach the state RERA authority (Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016) and/or a consumer commission. RERA allows a refund with interest, or interest for every month of delay.

Papers that usually help

  • Builder-buyer agreement / agreement for sale (all pages)
    The core document.
  • Booking form and allotment letter
    Delay can be counted from early documents in some cases.
  • All payment receipts and the builder's demand letters
    Basis of refund or interest.
  • Brochure, advertisements and promised amenities
    Shows misrepresentation.
  • RERA project page printout (registration, completion date, progress updates)
    Builder's own declarations to RERA.
  • Home loan sanction, tripartite agreement and EMI statements
    Needed if the builder stopped paying EMIs.
  • Emails and letters with the builder about delays
    Shows the history.
  • Possession offer letter and the demands attached
    Shows any unfair conditions.
  • Photos/videos of defects and an architect or engineer's report
    Supports claims for repair or compensation.
  • Insolvency/IRP public notice and your claim form
    Homebuyers must file claims with the resolution professional.

Questions Saboot will ask you

  • Where do things stand?
  • Is the project registered with RERA?
  • How much have you paid?
  • Is there a home loan or subvention scheme?
  • Has the builder raised new demands?
  • Has the builder got the occupancy/completion certificate?
  • Are other buyers in the project also affected?

Official sources
