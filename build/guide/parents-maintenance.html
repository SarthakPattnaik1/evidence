My children or relatives are not looking after me (senior citizen / parent)

Parents and senior citizens can claim monthly maintenance from children or heirs through a tribunal that must decide quickly. Property given on a promise of care can sometimes be taken back.

Answer a few questions and get your list →

What the law says

Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007: the tribunal (usually the SDM) can order up to ₹10,000 a month and should decide within 90 days. A transfer of property made on condition of care can be declared void if the care is refused (Section 23). Maintenance can also be claimed before a magistrate under the BNSS, without that cap.

Papers that usually help

  • Age proof (Aadhaar, pension book or birth certificate)
    The Act applies to parents and people 60+.
  • Proof of relationship to the children (birth certificates, ration card, family register)
    Who can be ordered to pay.
  • List of monthly expenses with medical bills
    Decides the amount.
  • What you know of their income, jobs, property
    The tribunal looks at their means.
  • Gift deed or transfer document, and anything showing it was on condition of care
    Needed to ask for the transfer to be declared void.
  • Medical reports, photos, witnesses of neglect or abuse
    Supports protection and eviction requests.
  • Application to the Maintenance Tribunal (SDM office)
    Starts the 90-day process — no lawyer is required.
