What the law says

NRIs and OCIs can own and inherit residential and commercial property in India. They generally cannot buy agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses, but can inherit them. You can act through an advocate and a power-of-attorney holder without living in India.

Papers that usually help

  • Title deed (sale deed, gift deed, allotment) — certified copy
    The foundation of any case.
  • Death certificate, legal heir/succession papers, will (if any)
    Inherited property needs the chain of succession.
  • Land/property records: mutation, khata/khatian, 7/12, property card
    Shows any changes made behind your back.
  • Encumbrance certificate (last 15–30 years)
    Detects fake sales or loans.
  • Property tax receipts and electricity/water bills in your name
    Strong supporting proof.
  • Your passport, OCI card and visa/residence proof abroad
    Needed for PoA, cases and registration.
  • PAN and Indian bank (NRO) account details
    Needed for rent, sale and court fees.
  • The power of attorney (and proof of any revocation)
    Misused or overbroad PoAs are a common cause of loss.
  • Revoke the misused PoA in writing, notify the sub-registrar and third parties
    Stops further misuse.
  • Rent agreement, rent received, and the tenant's police verification
    Basis for eviction or recovery.
  • Recent dated photos/video of the property (ask a trusted person)
    Shows encroachment or unauthorised use.
  • Messages and emails with the occupant, relative or caretaker
    Admissions and promises matter.
  • Copies of police, revenue or MADAD complaints and replies
    Shows you acted in time.
  • A fresh, specific PoA for your trusted representative/advocate
    Lets cases and complaints proceed while you're abroad.
