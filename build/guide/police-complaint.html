I want to file an FIR, or the police won't register my complaint

A clear written complaint with dates and documents makes an FIR far more likely — and more useful.

What the law says

Under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), police must register an FIR for cognizable offences, including a "zero FIR" at any station and e-FIRs. If they refuse, you can approach the Superintendent of Police and then a Magistrate.

Papers that usually help

  • A signed written complaint: who, what, when, where, and a list of attachments
    A clear written complaint is harder to ignore and becomes the basis of the FIR.
  • A short dated timeline of what happened
    Police and courts test complaints against dates.
  • Your ID and address proof
    Needed to register and follow up.
  • Supporting documents (bills, agreements, transaction records)
    Shows what happened.
  • Written request to preserve CCTV footage
    Footage is often overwritten within days.
  • Medical (MLC) report and injury photos
    Corroborates assault.
  • Witness names and contact details
    Helps investigation.
  • Details of the accused: name, phone, address, vehicle, bank account
    Speeds up investigation.
  • FIR copy and number
    Needed for follow-up, insurance and court.
  • Proof police refused (receipt, diary number, date, officer name)
    Needed to escalate to SP and Magistrate.
  • Complaint to the Superintendent of Police with postal proof
    Usually required before approaching a Magistrate.
