What the law says

Forums and rules depend on whether you are an "employee" or "worker", the type of employer and the state. Your advocate will check.

Papers that usually help

  • Appointment letter, offer email or contract
    States the salary and terms.
  • Salary slips
    Shows the agreed salary and any PF/tax deducted.
  • Bank statement showing past salary credits from the employer
    Shows the employment and the usual amount.
  • EPFO (UAN) passbook or ESI records
    Employer contributions are independent proof of employment.
  • Employee ID card, visiting card or uniform
    Supporting proof of employment.
  • Attendance, timesheets, rosters, work emails or work-group messages
    Shows work was done for the months claimed.
  • Records of cash wages (notebook entries, messages, co-workers' names)
    Cash wages need indirect proof.
  • Anything linking you to the employer (photos at work, delivery records, customer messages, co-workers)
    Without formal papers, several small pieces of proof can work together.
  • Your written request for salary and the employer's replies
    Shows the employer knew and did not pay.
  • Resignation, termination letter or relieving letter
    Final settlement dues depend on the exit date.
  • Employer's registered name and address (and GST/CIN if known)
    Needed for any notice or complaint.
  • Details of the contractor/agency and the principal company where you worked
    Both may have obligations to pay.
  • Your invoices, work orders and payment history (freelancers)
    Freelance claims are usually contract claims.
