Buying property — what to check, or I was cheated in a property deal

Most property fraud is avoidable with a title check before paying. If it has already happened, act fast — before the property is sold again.

What the law says

Title passes only by a registered sale deed; a power-of-attorney or agreement sale does not transfer ownership (Supreme Court, Suraj Lamp, 2011). New projects must be RERA-registered. Tax must be deducted at 1% where the price is ₹50 lakh or more.

Papers that usually help

  • Chain of title documents for about 30 years
    The core check — who owned it before and how it passed.
  • Encumbrance certificate
    Reveals mortgages and earlier sales.
  • Revenue / municipal records (mutation, khata, record of rights) and tax receipts
    Must match the title documents.
  • RERA registration, approved plan and occupancy/completion certificate
    Unregistered projects and missing OC are major risks.
  • Registered power of attorney and proof the owner is alive
    A cancelled PoA or deceased owner makes the sale invalid.
  • Death certificate, legal heir proof and consent of all heirs
    One missing heir can challenge the sale.
  • Land-use / conversion order and any purchase eligibility
    Agricultural land has state restrictions.
  • Agreement to sell, receipts and payment proof
    Basis for refund or a suit to enforce the sale.
  • Police complaint about the fraud
    Forgery and cheating are crimes; quick reporting matters.
