What the law says

Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 apply to platforms and sellers. Complaints can go to the National Consumer Helpline (1915), then the Consumer Commission on e-Jagriti within 2 years. From 1 January 2027, amended e-commerce rules also require platforms to take part in the National Consumer Helpline process and add new disclosure duties.

Papers that usually help

  • Order page, invoice and seller name
    Basic proof of the purchase.
  • Key dates: order, promised delivery, complaint, and any refund promise
    Shows delays against the platform's own promises and the 1-month redress rule.
  • Payment proof
    Needed for refund and chargeback.
  • Tracking page and delivery status
    Shows "delivered" when it was not.
  • Unboxing video / photos of what arrived
    The key proof for wrong, empty, fake or damaged items.
  • Product listing as it was when you ordered
    Proves it was not as described.
  • Complaint tickets, chat transcripts and replies
    Shows the platform had a chance to fix it.
  • Write to the platform's grievance officer (details on its website)
    They must acknowledge within 48 hours and redress within one month.
  • File on National Consumer Helpline (1915 / consumerhelpline.gov.in)
    Most e-commerce refunds are settled here.
  • Dispute the charge with your card issuer or bank
    Can reverse the payment if the platform fails.
