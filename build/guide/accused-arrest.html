What the law says

Under BNSS, for offences punishable up to 7 years police generally issue a notice to appear (Section 35) instead of arresting straight away. Anticipatory bail can be sought from the Sessions Court or High Court.

Papers that usually help

  • FIR or complaint copy
    Your defence starts here.
  • Get the FIR copy (online police portal or through an advocate)
    Needed before any application.
  • Police notice, summons or warrant — with envelope and date received
    Has dates you must not miss.
  • Documents that answer the allegations (agreements, payments, messages, alibi proof)
    Supports bail and defence.
  • Records of the earlier dispute with the complainant
    Shows context for the complaint.
  • ID, address proof, employment letter, medical records, dependants' details
    Courts consider these for bail.
  • Names of two people who could stand surety, with their ID and property/ salary proof
    Bail often requires sureties.
  • Details of any other cases
    Must be disclosed accurately in bail applications.

Questions Pehlo will ask you

  • What has happened so far?
  • What is the case about?
  • Do you have a copy of the FIR or complaint?
  • Do you know the complainant?
  • Have you ever been named in another case?
  • Is anything at stake if you are arrested — job, business, health, dependants?
