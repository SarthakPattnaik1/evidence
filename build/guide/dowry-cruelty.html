Dowry or cruelty case (498A) — filing one, or accused in one

This guide works for both sides: a woman facing cruelty or dowry demands, and family members accused in such a complaint.

What the law says

Cruelty by a husband or his relatives is now an offence under Section 85 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (formerly IPC 498A). The Dowry Prohibition Act also applies. Arrest is not automatic; courts have laid down safeguards.

Papers that usually help

  • Marriage certificate, wedding photos and invitation
    Basic background for either side.
  • Messages, calls or letters showing dowry/money demands
    Direct proof of demands.
  • Medical records and injury photos
    Independent proof.
  • List of stridhan and gifts with photos and bills
    Supports return of stridhan.
  • Names of people who saw incidents or heard demands
    Supports the account.
  • FIR copy, complaint and any police notice
    Everything turns on the exact allegations.
  • Proof of where each accused relative lives and works (rent, utility bills, employer letters)
    Courts look closely at general allegations against distant relatives.
  • Timeline of the marriage, with dates when she lived where
    Allegations are tested against dates.
  • Complete chats between spouses and families over the period
    Shows context on both sides.
  • Papers in the other cases (divorce, maintenance, DV)
    Statements in one case are compared in others.
  • Any written settlement terms
    Needed for quashing on settlement.
