What the law says

Shares depend on the personal law (Hindu Succession Act, Muslim personal law, Indian Succession Act), whether the property was ancestral or self-acquired, and whether there is a will. Since 2005, Hindu daughters are coparceners by birth.

Papers that usually help

  • Death certificate
    Required for every inheritance step.
  • Family tree with all legal heirs (names, ages, alive or not)
    Every claim starts with the list of heirs.
  • The will (original or copy), and any probate or registration details
    A will can be challenged on capacity, signatures or suspicious circumstances.
  • Records about the testator's health and situation when the will was signed
    Supports or answers a challenge.
  • Title deeds: sale deed, gift deed, partition deed, old family documents
    Shows ancestral or self-acquired character.
  • Land records: record of rights, khata/khatian, 7/12, jamabandi, mutation entries
    Shows who is recorded and any changes made.
  • Encumbrance certificate (to see if it was sold or mortgaged)
    Reveals sales or loans behind your back.
  • Property tax and electricity bills
    Supports possession and ownership claims.
  • Passbooks, policy and share details, nominee details, and the institution's reply
    A nominee generally holds money for the legal heirs.
  • Any release deed or family settlement you signed
    Central to whether a claim survives.
  • Proof of your relationship (birth certificate, school records, ID with parent's name)
    Required to claim.
  • Papers and orders in any pending case
    Avoids duplicate cases.
