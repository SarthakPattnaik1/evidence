What the law says

Spouses, children and parents can claim under different laws (including the BNSS, personal laws, the Domestic Violence Act, and the senior citizens' maintenance law).

Papers that usually help

  • Proof of the relationship (marriage certificate, birth certificate)
    The starting point of any claim.
  • The maintenance order and the record of payments received or missed
    Needed to recover arrears.
  • Papers in the pending case and the next date
    Keeps you prepared for the next hearing.
  • Records of the paying person's income, job, business, property or lifestyle
    The amount depends on their means.
  • Your own income proof, or a note that you have none
    Courts often require both sides to disclose income and assets in an affidavit.
  • Monthly expense records (rent, food, school fees, medical bills, loan EMIs)
    Supports the amount claimed.
  • Children's school fee receipts and medical records
    Children's costs are considered separately.
  • Messages showing support was refused or stopped
    Shows the need to approach the court.
  • The gift or transfer deed and any record of the promise of care
    May support a claim to undo the transfer.
  • Your income, liabilities, other dependants and payments already made
    The court will weigh your means and obligations.
  • Proof of payments already made
    May be adjusted against claims.

Questions Saboot will ask you

  • Who needs support, or who is being asked to pay?
  • Are you safe where you are living?
  • Is there already a maintenance order or case?
  • Do you have any lawful record of the paying person's income or assets?
  • Do you have your own income?
  • Do you have records of regular expenses?
  • Did you transfer property to the child or relative from whom you seek support?
  • Have you been paying anything already?
