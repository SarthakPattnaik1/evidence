What the law says

The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Act, 2026 (in force from 25 May 2026) removed the statutory right to self-identified gender: the District Magistrate now issues a certificate of identity after considering a designated medical board's recommendation. The amendment is under challenge in the Supreme Court. The 2019 Act's bar on discrimination in education, jobs and healthcare continues. Same-sex relations are not a crime (Navtej Johar, 2018), and courts grant police protection to couples facing threats.

Papers that usually help

  • Current ID documents (Aadhaar, PAN, school certificates, passport)
    Every change starts from these.
  • Application for a certificate of identity (National Portal for Transgender Persons / District Magistrate)
    Since May 2026 the District Magistrate decides after a medical board's recommendation — ask an advocate or support group before you apply.
  • Threat messages, recordings, names of people threatening
    Supports police or High Court protection.
  • Written request to the Superintendent of Police for protection
    Courts expect you to approach police first.
  • Messages, notices or rejection letters showing discrimination
    Basis for a complaint.
  • Age proof
    Police and courts check this first.
  • Affidavit of your name and gender, and any medical records you choose to share
    Applications and record updates ask for them; the medical board may ask for medical documents.
  • Contacts of a trusted friend, a queer support group and a lawyer
    Useful in an emergency and when going to police.
  • Dated notes of what happened and who was involved
    Makes complaints clear and believable.
