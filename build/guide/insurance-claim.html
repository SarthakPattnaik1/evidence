Insurance disputes turn on the policy wording and the exact reason for rejection. Let's get both.

What the law says

Complain first to the insurer's grievance officer. If not resolved, you can approach the Insurance Ombudsman (free) or a consumer commission. IRDAI rules set timelines for claim decisions.

Law checked 2026-10-30 Draft — not yet reviewed by a named advocate.

Papers that usually help

  • Policy document with schedule, terms and all renewal certificates
    The wording decides most disputes.
  • Proposal form (application) — ask the insurer for a copy
    Central to non-disclosure rejections.
  • Rejection / settlement letter with reasons
    What you must answer.
  • Claim form and acknowledgement with date
    Answers "late claim" objections.
  • Discharge summary, hospital bills, prescriptions, test reports
    Supports the claim amount.
  • Doctor's certificate on when the illness began
    Answers pre-existing disease objections.
  • FIR/accident report, survey report, repair estimate and invoices, photos
    Supports a motor claim.
  • Driving licence, RC and permit valid on the accident date
    Answers licence objections.
  • Death certificate, medical records of last illness, nominee ID
    Needed for life claims.
  • Grievance complaint and insurer's reply
    Needed for Ombudsman.
  • File a written grievance with the insurer and keep the reference
    First step before Ombudsman.
  • Renewal and portability records showing continuous cover
    Limits non-disclosure rejections after long cover.

Questions Saboot will ask you

  • What kind of insurance?
  • What happened to the claim?
  • What reason did they give?
  • Have you complained to the insurer's grievance cell in writing?
  • Did they reply within 30 days?
  • How long have you held the policy continuously (including renewals/portability)?
  • Did an agent or bank fill the proposal form for you?
