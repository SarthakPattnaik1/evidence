Tenant–landlord dispute: eviction, rent, or tenant not leaving

Both tenants and landlords use this guide. Eviction must follow due process — locks, power cuts and threats are not a lawful shortcut.

What the law says

Tenancy is governed by state rent laws (some states follow the Model Tenancy Act), the rent agreement, and the Transfer of Property Act.

Papers that usually help

  • Rent agreement and any renewals
    Starting point for both sides.
  • Rent payment records for the whole tenancy
    Decides default claims.
  • Proof of tenancy without agreement (rent receipts, address proofs, police verification, utility bills)
    Needed when nothing is written.
  • The notice (sent or received) with postal proof
    Defective notices are a common defence.
  • Photos/videos and complaints about utilities cut or locks changed
    Supports urgent court or police action.
  • Proof of the owner's genuine need (family size, current housing, business plans)
    A common eviction ground that must be proved.
  • Ownership papers of the property
    Landlords must show their right to let/evict.
  • Photos of the property's condition and repair bills
    Answers damage claims.
  • Police verification form
    Supports the tenancy record.
  • Messages between landlord and tenant
    Shows threats, promises or admissions.
