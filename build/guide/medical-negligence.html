Accidents

Medical negligence, hospital overbilling, or hospital refusing records or discharge

Your medical records are the case. Hospitals must give copies on request, and a doctor is liable only if care fell well below accepted standards — an independent medical opinion usually decides it.

What the law says

Patients or attendants can get copies of records within 72 hours of a request (Indian Medical Council Regulations, 2002). Negligence claims go to the Consumer Commission (district up to ₹50 lakh, state up to ₹2 crore) within 2 years. Criminal cases against doctors need gross negligence and usually an independent medical opinion first (Jacob Mathew, 2005).

Papers that usually help

  • Complete case file: admission notes, consent forms, treatment and nursing charts, test reports, discharge summary
    The core evidence in every medical case.
  • All bills, estimates and payment receipts
    Proves overbilling and your loss.
  • Patient ID / admission number, and proof of your relationship if you are not the patient
    Needed to request records and to file a claim.
  • Dated timeline: symptoms, what doctors said, what was done, what went wrong
    Experts and commissions need a clear sequence.
  • Written opinion from an independent doctor
    Commissions and police rely on expert opinion.
  • Records of later treatment that fixed or found the problem
    Shows the consequence of the negligence.
  • Post-mortem report and death certificate
    Key in death cases.
  • Income proof and extra costs caused
    Decides compensation.
  • Written request to the hospital for records
    Refusal after 72 hours is itself a complaint.
