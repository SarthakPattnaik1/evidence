Making a will, gift deed or release deed — passing property to family

A will takes effect after death and can be changed any time. A gift deed works now and is hard to undo. Choose with care.

What the law says

A will must be in writing, signed by the person making it and attested by two witnesses (Indian Succession Act, 1925); registration is optional but helps. A gift of immovable property must be by a registered deed (Transfer of Property Act, 1882) and stamp duty applies, often reduced for close family. A release/relinquishment deed by a co-owner must also be registered. Muslims can will away only up to one-third without heirs’ consent. Under the Senior Citizens Act, 2007, a gift made on the condition of being cared for can be cancelled by the tribunal if that care is not given.

Papers that usually help

  • Title documents for each property (sale deed, mutation, tax receipts)
    A will or gift cannot pass more than you own.
  • List of assets with account / folio / policy numbers and current nominees
    Avoids gaps and conflicts with nominations.
  • Names, ages and addresses of family members and beneficiaries
    Clear identification avoids challenges.
  • ID proof of you and the beneficiaries
    Needed for registration.
  • Two adult witnesses who are not beneficiaries
    A will without two witnesses can fail.
  • A doctor’s note that you are of sound mind (helpful if older or unwell)
    The most common challenge to a will is mental capacity.
  • Documents showing the co-ownership and the other co-owners’ details
    A release deed must describe the share given up.
  • The registered gift deed and proof of the care condition
    Needed to cancel a gift under the Senior Citizens Act.
  • Check stamp duty and registration fee for family gifts in your state
    Many states charge much less for gifts to close family.
