Someone posing as police, CBI, customs or a court threatened me or took money ("digital arrest")

No police, CBI, ED, customs officer or judge ever arrests anyone over a video call or asks for money to "verify" it. Reporting fast is what gives money a chance of being frozen.

What the law says

There is no "digital arrest" under Indian law. The Supreme Court asked the CBI to coordinate a pan-India probe into these scams in late 2025. Report on 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in; money moved to mule accounts can sometimes be frozen and later released by court order.

Papers that usually help

  • Phone numbers, video-call IDs and names they used
    Police trace and block these numbers and accounts.
  • Fake notices, "warrants" or ID cards they sent
    Shows the fraud and helps identify the gang.
  • Every transaction: date, time, amount, UTR and the receiving account
    Banks freeze the receiving accounts using these details.
  • 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in acknowledgement number
    Needed for bank, police and court steps.
  • FIR or police complaint copy
    Needed to ask the court to release frozen money back to you.
  • Written complaint to your bank, with reference number
    Starts the bank's own fraud process and record.
  • Call 1930 or file on cybercrime.gov.in now
    This is how freezes start.
  • Report the number on Sanchar Saathi (Chakshu)
    Helps block it so others are not targeted.
  • Change passwords, uninstall any app they made you install, and tell your bank about shared details
    Shared details can be used again.
