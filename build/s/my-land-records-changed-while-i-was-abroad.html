My land records changed while I was abroad

What usually matters in this situation, and the papers to keep ready before you meet an advocate or free legal aid (call 15100).

Your situation

  • What is happening with the property? Records changed into someone else's name

Papers to gather

  • Title deed (sale deed, gift deed, allotment) — certified copy
    The foundation of any case. Where: Your records; certified copy from the sub-registrar (many states allow online request).
  • Land/property records: mutation, khata/khatian, 7/12, property card
    Shows any changes made behind your back. Where: State land records portal; municipal property tax portal.
  • Encumbrance certificate (last 15–30 years)
    Detects fake sales or loans. Where: Sub-registrar / state portal.
  • Property tax receipts and electricity/water bills in your name
    Strong supporting proof. Where: Municipality and utility portals.
  • Your passport, OCI card and visa/residence proof abroad
    Needed for PoA, cases and registration. Where: Your documents.
  • PAN and Indian bank (NRO) account details
    Needed for rent, sale and court fees. Where: Your records.
  • Recent dated photos/video of the property (ask a trusted person)
    Shows encroachment or unauthorised use. Where: Trusted friend, neighbour or local advocate.
  • Messages and emails with the occupant, relative or caretaker
    Admissions and promises matter. Where: Your phone and email.

You may also need, depending on your answers

  • Death certificate, legal heir/succession papers, will (if any) — Inherited property needs the chain of succession.
  • The power of attorney (and proof of any revocation) — Misused or overbroad PoAs are a common cause of loss.
  • Revoke the misused PoA in writing, notify the sub-registrar and third parties — Stops further misuse.
  • Copies of police, revenue or MADAD complaints and replies — Shows you acted in time.
  • A fresh, specific PoA for your trusted representative/advocate — Lets cases and complaints proceed while you're abroad.

What the law says

NRIs and OCIs can own and inherit residential and commercial property in India. They generally cannot buy agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses, but can inherit them. You can act through an advocate and a power-of-attorney holder without living in India.
