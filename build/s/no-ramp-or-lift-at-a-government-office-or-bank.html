No ramp or lift at a government office or bank

What usually matters in this situation, and the papers to keep ready before you meet an advocate or free legal aid (call 15100).

Your situation

  • What happened? A building, bank, transport or website is not accessible

Where you stand

  • In your favour You are entitled to a free lawyer whatever your income — call 15100.
  • In your favour A complaint to the Disabilities Commissioner is free, needs no lawyer and can be filed online or by email.

Papers to gather

  • UDID card or disability certificate
    Needed for every right, reservation and free legal aid. Where: swavlambancard.gov.in, or the district medical board.
  • The refusal — letter, email, message or a note of what was said, by whom and when
    The core proof of discrimination. Where: Your records.
  • Photos or video of the barrier (steps, no ramp, no lift, inaccessible website screenshots)
    Shows the barrier clearly. Where: Your phone.
  • A short list of the support you need (ramp, interpreter, large print, scribe, extra time)
    Denying reasonable accommodation is discrimination under the Act. Where: Write it yourself.

You may also need, depending on your answers

  • Send a written complaint to the office or company and keep the receipt — Commissioners and courts ask whether you complained first.

What the law says

The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 bars discrimination on the ground of disability, and denying reasonable accommodation is discrimination. Government establishments cannot discriminate in employment or promotion (s.20) and must reserve at least 4% of posts for persons with benchmark disabilities (40% or more) (s.34); government and aided higher-education institutions must reserve at least 5% of seats with 5 years’ age relaxation (s.32). Courts, police and commissions must be accessible (s.12). Complaints go free of charge to the Chief Commissioner (central bodies and others) or the State Commissioner (state government bodies) (ss.75, 80) — by post, email or online, without a lawyer or a personal visit. Intentionally insulting or intimidating a person with disability in public view to humiliate them is punishable with 6 months to 5 years (s.92). Every person with a disability is entitled to free legal aid (Legal Services Authorities Act, s.12(d)).
