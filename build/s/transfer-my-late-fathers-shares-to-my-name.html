Your situation

  • Who is the problem with? A listed company or its registrar (shares, dividend)
  • What went wrong? Share transfer, transmission or dividend

Where you stand

  • In your favour The entity must reply on SCORES within 21 days, and you can ask for two levels of review.
  • Next step For money claims, SEBI’s online dispute resolution offers conciliation and then arbitration.

Papers to gather

  • Contract notes and the trade / transaction statement for the period
    Shows the disputed trades or transactions. Where: Broker app or email; NSDL/CDSL statement.
  • Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) from NSDL or CDSL
    Independent record of shares and mutual funds. Where: NSDL / CDSL email, or request online.
  • Bank statement showing money paid in and out
    Matches money to the account. Where: Your bank.
  • Share certificates, folio numbers, death certificate and succession papers (for transmission)
    Needed for transfer, transmission and old dividends. Where: Family papers; registrar.

You may also need, depending on your answers

  • Your complaint to the entity and their reply, with ticket number — SCORES and SEBI review rely on it.
  • Write to the broker / fund / company first, then file on SCORES — Starts the 21-day clock.

What the law says

SEBI’s SCORES 2.0 sends your complaint straight to the broker, depository participant, mutual fund, registrar or listed company, which must resolve it and file an Action Taken Report within 21 calendar days. If you are not satisfied you can ask for a first review within 15 days (by the stock exchange or depository), and then a second review by SEBI. A complaint must be filed within one year of the problem. Money disputes can go to SEBI’s online dispute resolution (SMART ODR) for conciliation and arbitration. Unregistered “tip” sellers and fake trading apps are frauds — report them to 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in.
